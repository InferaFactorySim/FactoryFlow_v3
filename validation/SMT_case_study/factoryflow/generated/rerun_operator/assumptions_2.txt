1.  The board source, which never runs out and always has the next stack ready, is modeled as a `BatchSource` with an `inter_arrival_time` of 0, meaning a new stack is available immediately after the previous one is fully dispatched.
2.  The screen printer's automatic cleaning cycle is modeled as a service that is triggered after every second board, has a duration of 28 seconds, and does not require an operator.
3.  The screen printer's solder and adhesive top-up is modeled as two separate, sequential service tasks, each requiring the operator for 60 seconds, with both being requested after every 100 boards are processed.
4.  The buffering module, which "just passes them straight through", is modeled as a `Machine` with a `processing_delay` of 0.
5.  The reflow oven is assumed to power down when it becomes idle (i.e., has no boards inside and no boards waiting to enter) and requires the full one-hour warm-up period before it can process the next board.
6.  When multiple machines request service from the operator, the requests are served in a First-In, First-Out (FIFO) order.
7.  All machines, except for the reflow oven whose capacity is specified, are assumed to process one board at a time, giving them a `work_capacity` of 1.
8.  The simulation starts with all machines idle, all buffers empty, and the reflow oven in its "off" (cold) state.
9.  For the screen printer and placement machines, the specified "idle" power consumption is assumed to apply to all non-processing states, including waiting for an operator or being blocked by a downstream component.
