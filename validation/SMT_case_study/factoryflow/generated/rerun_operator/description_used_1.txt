This is our SMT line for assembling printed circuit boards. The boards come to the head
of the line in stacks of sixteen. A whole stack is loaded at once and then the boards go
into the line one at a time; we always have the next stack ready and waiting at the head
while the current one is being fed, and we never run out of boards. Call the stack feed
source_1.

The first machine is the line loader, line_loader. It takes the boards off the stack one
by one and puts them onto the line. It handles one board every two seconds.

Next is the screen printer, screen_printer, which puts the solder paste onto the board.
It does one board at a time and takes eighteen seconds each. After every second board it
runs a cleaning cycle, which takes twenty-eight seconds; the machine does this by itself
and nobody has to attend to it. It is filled with five hundred grams of solder and five
hundred grams of adhesive, and each board uses five grams of each, so after a hundred
boards an operator has to top up both. The solder and the adhesive are topped up as two
separate jobs of sixty seconds each, and the printer is stopped while it waits.

From the printer the boards travel to the first placement machine on a belt,
belt_sp_pp1. The belt has three board positions on it and moves along by one position
every second. It does not stack boards up: if the board at the far end is not picked off,
the whole belt stops.

The first placement machine, pick_and_place_1, fits the components onto the board from
reels. It takes eighty-five seconds a board and it is the slowest thing on the line.
Every fifty boards a reel has to be changed, which takes the operator sixty seconds, and
the machine stands idle until that is done.

The second placement machine, pick_and_place_2, does the rest of the components at fifty
seconds a board, and it also needs a reel change every fifty boards, again sixty seconds.

There is one operator on the line, operator_1, who does all the topping up and all the
reel changes. They can only do one job at a time, so if a machine needs attention while
they are busy on another, that machine has to wait its turn.

After the second placement machine the boards pass the buffering module,
buffering_module, which in this setup just passes them straight through, and then go on
a second belt, belt_bm_rfo, the same as the first one: three positions, one second per
position, and it stops if the end board is not taken off.

Last is the reflow oven, reflow_oven. It is a heated chamber with a belt running through
it, where the solder paste melts and makes the joints. Ten boards fit inside it at once
and a board takes fifty seconds to pass through. The oven has to be brought up to
temperature before it can take any boards at all, and from cold that takes an hour.

Finished boards come off the oven and are collected at the end of the line; call the
collection point sink_1.

Between each machine there is a single board position where one board waits for the next
machine to be free. Going down the line these are b0 between source_1 and the line loader, b1
between the line loader and the printer, b2 between the two placement machines, b3
between the second placement machine and the buffering module, and b4 between the oven
and the collection point.

For the electricity: the screen printer draws a hundred watts when it is sitting idle,
waiting for a top-up, or waiting to pass a board on, five hundred watts while it is
printing, and a thousand watts while it is cleaning. Each placement machine draws a
hundred watts when idle, waiting for a reel change, or waiting to pass a board on, and
five hundred watts while it is placing. The buffering module draws two hundred and fifty
watts all the time. The oven draws three hundred and twenty watts when it is switched
off, thirty-three thousand watts while it is heating up, and twenty-five thousand eight
hundred watts once it is at temperature, whether or not there are boards inside. The line
loader, the two belts and the single board positions use no electricity worth counting,
so the energy figures cover only the printer, the two placement machines, the buffering
module and the oven.
